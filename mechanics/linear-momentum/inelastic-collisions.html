Inelastic Collisions

< Return

Summary

A perfectly inelastic collision is when the coefficient of restitution is equal to zero

Both objects stick together as one mass after collision

A more general inelastic collision has coefficient of restitution between one and zero

Equations

vMf=vmf
mvm0+MvM0m+M=vf

While it is useful to have the conservation of momentum, with the coefficient of restitution, we can simplify some special cases. One special case we often see is when two objects collide and stick to each other, which we call as the following.

Perfectly inelastic collision - a collision where e=0

Before, we note that a perfectly inelastic collision occurs when the speed of seperation is zero. Thus, after a perfectly inelastic collision, both objects move together as one (i.e., they stick together).

Mathematically, we can see it like this. We will use our previous notation before, with masses m and M. This equation arrives with what we have seen before.

0=|vmf−vMf||vm0−vM0|
0=|vmf−vMf|
0=vmf−vMf
vMf=vmf

Thus, we can see that the velocity of the two masses are the same, meaning they both move at the same speed. This aligns with we see, where they move at one speed.

Exercise 1a

Two objects are moving and collide with velocities vm0 and vM0 with masses m and M respectively. They collide in an inelastic collision. Show that the final velocity is computed by the following equation.

mvm0+MvM0m+M=vf

Solution

We first write out the conservation of momentum for the two objects.

p0=pf
pm0+pM0=pmf+pMf
mvm0+MvM0=mvmf+MvMf

We note the above relation with an inelastic collision, so we substitute it in.

mvm0+MvM0=mvmf+Mvmf
mvm0+MvM0=vmf(m+M)
mvm0+MvM0m+M=vmf

If we use a different symbol for the final velocity, dropping a subscript since both move at the same velocity anyway, we have the above equation.

Exercise 1b

Let the two objects have the same mass m yet different velocities. Show then that the equation for the final velocity of the two particles is below.

vm0+vM02=vf

Solution

We will write out the previous equation, and have all of the objects have the same mass.

mvm0+MvM0m+M=vf
mvm0+mvM0m+m=vf
m(vm0+mvM0)2m=vf
vm0+vM02=vf

We can extend this fact to two-dimensions by noting the conservation of momentum in two dimensions. Thus, in a perfectly inelastic collision, we have the following facts.

vMfx=vmfx
vMfy=vmfy

The exercise below uses the above facts. However, you do need one fact from trigonometry, which we write below.

sin²(θ)+cos²(θ)=1

Exercise 2

(HRK E6.33) After a totally inelastic collision, two objects of the same mass and initial speed are found to move away together at half their initial speed. Find the angle between the initial velocities of the objects.

Solution

Without loss of generality, let's let the first object move completely horizontal such that it has no vertical speed. Thus, we only need to care then about the angle of the second object.

We will use θ for the initial angle of the second object and φ for the angle the two objects move at.

p0x=pfx
mv+mvcos(θ)=2mv2cos(φ)
1+cos(θ)=cos(φ)
cos(θ)=cos(φ)−1

For the vertical component, we have about the same scenario.

p0y=pfy
mvsin(θ)=2mv2sin(φ)
sin(θ)=sin(φ)

Now, let's try squaring both of the equations together.

cos²(θ)=cos²(φ)−2cos(φ)+1
sin²(θ)=sin²(φ)

We add both together.

cos²(θ)+sin²(θ)=cos²(φ)−2cos(φ)+1+sin²(φ)
1=1−2cos(φ)+1
12=cos(φ)

Thus, we substitute it to the value above.

cos(θ)=12−1
cos(θ)=−12

This gives us that the value of θ is 120°.

One thing to note is that perfectly inelastic collisions are a "subtype" of the more general type of collision noted below.

Inelastic collision - a collision where e<1

In these collisions, while the objects don't stick together, they don't completely rebound at the same speed they came at. In the next tutorial, we will analyze these rebounds in the special case where they are at the same speed.
