Equations

Mdvdt=Fext+vreldMdt
Δv=FextMΔt+vrelln(M0M)

Previously, we derived the following equation for the force acting on an object with variable mass.

Mdvdt=Fext+vreldMdt

We can write the above equation like the following as well, noting that the derivative of velocity with respect to time is acceleration.

Ma=Fext+vreldMdt
Fnet=Fext+vreldMdt

Here, this force has various names. We will note two here.

Thrust - the force on a rocket due to the ejection of propellant
Recoil - a force on an object by the ejection of mass from it

Thrust is the more common term used for rocket propulsion, while recoil can be used for ballistic purposes such as when firing a gun or a cannon. In those instances, a huge mass is thrown out, causing the assembly to jolt backward.

Exercise 1a

(HRK E7.20a) A rocket engine in deep space, where there is virtually no gravity, consumes fuel at the rate of 480 kg/s, and the exhaust speed is 3.27 km/s. Find the thrust of the rocket engine.

Solution

We use the equation above directly. We note no external forces; there is virtually no gravity.

Fthrust=Fext+vrpdMdt
Fthrust=vrpdMdt
Fthrust=(3.27km/s)(480kg/s)

However, to express this in Newtons, we do have to convert the kilometers to meters.

Fthrust=(32700m/s)(480kg/s)=156960000N

This is approximately 1.60×108N.

We can also use the original equation to get the change in velocity of an object after losing/gaining fuel.

Mdvdt=Fext+vreldMdt
dvdt=FextM+vrelMdMdt

Here, we will multiply both sides by dt to clean everything up.

dv=FextMdt+vreldMM

Now, we integrate all sides with respect to time. We choose the bounds of integration from initial time to final time. Further, we will make the assumption that there is a constant external force (e.g. gravity near the Earth's surface).

∫v0vdv=∫t0tFextMdt+∫M0MvreldMM
∫v0vdv=FextM∫t0tdt+vrel∫M0MdMM

We now evaluate the integrals. We will note the following result from calculus, that ∫1x=ln(x)+C.

1|v0v=FextM1|t0t+vrelln(M)|M0M
Δv=FextMΔt+vrel[ln(M)−ln(M0)]
Δv=FextMΔt+vrelln(M0M)

If we drop out the external force (let's say in deep space, where gravity is negligible), we have the following equation, found by Konstantin Tsiolkovsky in 1987. It is sometimes called the rocket equation.

Δv=vrelln(M0M)

(HRK E7.20c) The rocket engine above starts at rest and has a mass of 2.55×105 kg, of which 1.81×105 kg is fuel. The engine is fired for 250 s. What is the final speed attained?

Problem 1

(HRK P7.8) A rocket sled with a mass of 2870 kg moves at 252 m/s on a set of rails. At a certain point, a scoop on the sled dips into a trough of water located between the tracks and scoops water into an empty tank on the sled. Determine the speed of the sled after 917 kg of water have been scooped up.

Solution

Be careful! You should not use the rocket equation here. The relative velocity of propulsion is zero here since the water is at rest. Thus, instead of using that, we will just note that this is an inelastic collision. Using our equation:

mvm0+MvM0m+M=vf
(2870kg)(252m/s)917kg+2870kg=191m/s

One such example of a problem where there is an external force are rockets which need to launch. Here, the thrust has to surpass the weight of the rocket. Plugging in, we have the following relation:

Δv=−wMΔt+vrelln(M0M)
Δv=vrelln(M0M)−MgMΔt
Δv=vrelln(M0M)−gΔt

Exercise 3

(HRK P7.10b) A 5860-kg rocket is set for vertical firing. The exhaust speed is 1.17 km/s. How much gas must be ejected each second to supply the thrust needed to give the rocket an initial upward acceleration of 18.3 m/s²?

Solution

Note here that the rocket is still on Earth; thus, the force of weight has to be account for.

Mdvdt=Fext+vreldMdt
Mdvdt=−w+vreldMdt
Mdvdt=−Mg+vreldMdt

We solve for dMdt.

Mdvdt+Mg=+vreldMdt
Mdvdt+Mgvrel=dMdt

Plug in known values.

(5860kg)(18.3m/s²)+(5860kg)(9.8m/s²)(1170m/s)≈141kg/s=dMdt

Exercise 4

(LSM P9.78) A rocket takes off from Earth and reaches a speed of 100 m/s in 10.0 s. If the exhaust speed is 1500 m/s and the mass of fuel burned is 100 kg, what was the initial mass of the rocket?

Solution

Gravity plays a role here, so we have to account for it.

Δv=vrelln(M0M)−gΔt

Here, we note that the burned mass is 100 kg, so now we can solve for the initial mass.

Δv=vrelln(M0M0−100)−gΔt
Δv+gΔt=vrelln(M0M0−100)
Δv+gΔtvrel=ln(M0M0−100)
eΔv+gΔtvrel=M0M0−100
eΔv+gΔtvrel(M0−100)=M0
eΔv+gΔtvrelM0−eΔv+gΔtvrel100=M0
eΔv+gΔtvrelM0−M0=eΔv+gΔtvrel100
M0(eΔv+gΔtvrel−1)=100eΔv+gΔtvrel
M0=100eΔv+gΔtvreleΔv+gΔtvrel−1

Finally, we now just plug in the values we have.

M0=100e(100m/s)+(9.8m/s²)(10.0s)(1500m/s)e(100m/s)+(9.8m/s²)(10.0s)(1500m/s)−1
M0≈800kg
